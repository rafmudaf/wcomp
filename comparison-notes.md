




# Jiménez wake-deflection model comparison

## Scope

This note records the investigation of the small differences between the
FLORIS, FOXES, and PyWake Jiménez wake-deflection results in the TORQUE 2024
comparison cases.

The cases use:

- Jensen velocity deficit model
- Jiménez wake deflection model
- `beta = 0.1`
- One-turbine yaw: `10°`
- Four-turbine yaw: `[10°, 10°, 0°, 0°]`
- IEA 15 MW turbine, `D = 240 m`, wind speed `9.8 m/s`

The comparison is generated by
[`wcomp.dataset`](../wcomp/dataset.py). The solver adapters are
[FLORIS](../wcomp/floris_interface.py),
[FOXES](../wcomp/foxes_interface.py), and
[PyWake](../wcomp/pywake_interface.py).

## Parameter mapping

The windIO case defines the Jiménez coefficient as:

```yaml
deflection:
  name: jimenez
  parameters:
    beta: 0.1
```

FOXES and PyWake use this value directly as `beta=0.1`. FLORIS calls the
corresponding coefficient `kd`, but its implementation uses a different
normalization in the downstream term. The adapter therefore converts:

```text
kd = beta / 2 = 0.05
```

This conversion is implemented in
[`WCompFloris._create_floris_dict`](../wcomp/floris_interface.py) and covered
by [`test_jimenez_beta_is_converted_to_floris_kd`](../tests/test_floris_interface.py).

## Deflection equations

The installed FLORIS implementation evaluates the Jiménez displacement in
closed form. Its wake-expansion factor is:

```text
q_FLORIS = 1 + 2 * kd * x / D
```

FOXES integrates the local deflection angle numerically using 10 m streamwise
steps. PyWake integrates the same rate with a 20-point logarithmically spaced
streamwise grid. Their expansion factor is:

```text
q_FOXES/PyWake = 1 + beta * x / D
```

With `kd=beta/2`, the FLORIS and FOXES/PyWake expansion factors are
mathematically aligned. Before this conversion, FLORIS expanded the wake
approximately twice as quickly and produced increasingly smaller downstream
displacements.

## Thrust-coefficient treatment

Jiménez deflection is proportional to the source-turbine thrust coefficient,
so differences in the runtime `Ct` value also affect the centerline.

For the one-turbine case at `9.8 m/s` and `10°` yaw, the observed runtime
values were:

| Software | Runtime `Ct` | Treatment |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| FLORIS | 0.791347 | Applies its cosine yaw correction to the turbine model |
| FOXES | 0.803593 | Remains close to the interpolated unyawed turbine curve |
| PyWake | 0.779399 | Evaluates at projected wind speed and applies its yaw correction |

The interpolated input turbine curve is approximately `Ct=0.803555` at
`9.8 m/s`. These differences are independent of the `beta`/`kd` normalization
and explain most of the residual separation after the FLORIS conversion.

## Numerical integration

The integration method is a smaller contributor:

- FLORIS: closed-form displacement.
- FOXES: 10 m step integration.
- PyWake: 20-point logarithmic trapezoidal integration.

Using a common `Ct` and `beta`, the estimated FOXES/PyWake integration
difference is approximately `0.06 m` at 1D, `0.16 m` at 5D, `0.17 m` at 10D,
and `0.08 m` at 20D. This is much smaller than the pre-correction FLORIS
difference.

## Corrected comparison results

After changing the FLORIS mapping to `kd=beta/2` and regenerating both cases,
the horizontal-contour metrics are:

| Scenario | Pair | RMSE | Maximum absolute difference |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| One turbine | FLORIS–FOXES | 0.040931 | 1.255553 |
| One turbine | FLORIS–PyWake | 0.165613 | 5.197115 |
| One turbine | FOXES–PyWake | 0.174689 | 5.197115 |
| Four turbines | FLORIS–FOXES | 0.176966 | 2.873472 |
| Four turbines | FLORIS–PyWake | 0.244691 | 5.197115 |
| Four turbines | FOXES–PyWake | 0.168920 | 5.197115 |

The corrected FLORIS/FOXES one-turbine result is close. The remaining
FLORIS/PyWake and FOXES/PyWake differences are primarily consistent with
their different yaw-dependent `Ct` conventions, with smaller contributions
from integration and the accumulated multi-turbine wake calculation.

## Conclusions and next controlled test

The differences rank as follows:

1. FLORIS's `kd` normalization, now corrected in the adapter.
2. Different yaw-dependent thrust-coefficient conventions.
3. Numerical integration and multi-turbine accumulation.

The next controlled comparison should hold `Ct` fixed across all three
solvers while retaining `beta=0.1` and the corrected FLORIS `kd=0.05`. That
will isolate the remaining deflection-equation and integration differences
from turbine-model differences.

# Wake-deficit reference wind speed

## Summary

The four-turbine Jensen case showed FLORIS disagreeing with FOXES and PyWake
at turbines 3 and 4, where more than one wake overlaps. The cause is which
wind speed scales each upstream wake's deficit. It is not the deficit
geometry, induction, or rotor averaging: at turbine 2, which sees a single
wake, all three agree to five digits (`8.43589 m/s`).

In all three solvers the combined deficit is subtracted from the freestream
speed at the target, and the individual deficits are combined in quadrature:

```text
U_target = U_freestream - sqrt(sum_j deficit_j^2)
```

What differs is the size of each individual `deficit_j`:

| Software | Setting | `deficit_j` for a Jensen wake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FLORIS | `sosfs`, deficit times `u_initial` | `2a * C(x) * U_freestream` |
| PyWake (previously) | `use_effective_ws=True` | `2a * C(x) * U_waked at source turbine` |
| FOXES (previously) | `ws_quadratic` | `2a * C(x) * U_waked at source turbine` |

Because turbine 2 sees only `8.44 m/s`, the local convention makes its wake
weaker than FLORIS's, which still scales by `9.8 m/s`. Turbine 1 is unaffected
since its local and freestream speeds are the same.

## Evidence

Four-turbine Jensen turbine inflow speeds (`m/s`) before alignment:

| Turbine | FLORIS | FOXES | PyWake |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 9.8000 | 9.8000 | 9.8000 |
| 2 | 8.4359 | 8.4359 | 8.4359 |
| 3 | 8.3061 | 8.4775 | 8.4775 |
| 4 | 8.2670 | 8.4643 | 8.4643 |

A hand calculation reproduces both columns. With `a = 0.278`, `C = 0.25` at 5D
and `C = 0.111` at 10D, the turbine 3 deficit is
`hypot(0.606, 0.557*0.25*9.8) = 1.493` for FLORIS and
`hypot(0.606, 0.557*0.25*8.436) = 1.322` for the local convention.

Switching the convention in a controlled run, with nothing else changed,
removed the difference. FOXES with ambient scaling matched FLORIS over the
whole streamwise profile (RMSE below 1e-4), and PyWake with
`use_effective_ws=False` matched FLORIS turbine speeds to 1e-5.

## Change

The adapters now follow FLORIS:

- [PyWake](../wcomp/pywake_interface.py): `use_effective_ws=False`.
- [FOXES](../wcomp/foxes_interface.py): `superposition="ws_quadratic_amb_target"`.

FOXES offers two ambient variants. `ws_quadratic_amb` scales by the source
turbine's ambient rotor-effective speed, which FOXES reduces by the yaw
projection (`~9.63 m/s` at a `10°` yawed turbine instead of `9.8 m/s`). That
left a residual in the Jiménez cases. `ws_quadratic_amb_target` scales by the
ambient speed at the target point. For the uniform, shear-free inflow used
here this equals FLORIS's `u_initial` for yawed and unyawed turbines alike. It
would differ from FLORIS in sheared or non-uniform inflow.

The convention applies to every case, since FLORIS scales by `u_initial` in its
Jensen, Gauss and TurbOPark velocity models. Single-turbine results are
unchanged because a lone wake sees the freestream either way.

## Results after alignment

Four-turbine RMSE (`m/s`) before to after, excluding the sample points
described below:

| Case | Pair | Streamwise | Horizontal contour |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Jensen | FLORIS–FOXES | 0.264 to 0.000 | 0.192 to 0.063 |
| Jensen | FLORIS–PyWake | 0.264 to 0.000 | 0.190 to 0.058 |
| Jiménez | FLORIS–FOXES | 0.256 to 0.052 | 0.181 to 0.055 |
| Jiménez | FLORIS–PyWake | 0.263 to 0.024 | 0.194 to 0.071 |
| TurbOPark | FLORIS–FOXES | 1.285 to 0.237 | 0.570 to 0.052 |
| TurbOPark | FLORIS–PyWake | 1.300 to 0.237 | 0.576 to 0.051 |
| Bastankhah 2016 | FLORIS–FOXES | 1.301 to 1.284 | 0.554 to 0.807 |
| Bastankhah 2016 deflection | FLORIS–FOXES | 1.326 to 1.211 | 0.568 to 0.820 |
| Bastankhah 2014 | FOXES–PyWake | 0.010 to 0.014 | 0.004 to 0.006 |

FOXES–PyWake agreement falls slightly for the Jiménez case (streamwise
`0.035` to `0.076`). The likely reason is the yaw-dependent `Ct` difference
between FOXES and PyWake, which the earlier shared local scaling may have
partly masked. This was not isolated.

The two Bastankhah 2016 cases got worse in the contour metric. This is not the
superposition convention. Turbine 2 sees a single wake and already differs
(`6.98 m/s` FLORIS, `6.60 m/s` FOXES), and those one-wake values are unchanged
by this work. FLORIS also adds wake turbulence that FOXES does not, which raises
the downstream FLORIS speeds. With wake-added turbulence disabled in FLORIS the
four-turbine contour RMSE against FOXES was `0.606` rather than `0.806`. The
single-wake Bastankhah 2016 mismatch, visible in the one-turbine case at an
RMSE of `0.77 m/s`, needs its own investigation.

## Sampling artifacts in the metrics

Two kinds of sample points inflate the reported maxima and RMSE independent of
the models:

- **Rotor-plane points.** A point exactly at a turbine's streamwise position
  sees PyWake add that turbine's own undecayed deficit. The 100-point
  streamwise line lands exactly on 15D, and the contour grid lands on each
  turbine plane. In the Jensen case this one point is the entire
  FOXES–PyWake streamwise RMSE, which is `0.414` with it and `0.000` without.
  The maximum values in `metrics.json` therefore still show `4.1 m/s` for the
  Jensen FLORIS–PyWake and FOXES–PyWake streamwise pairs.
- **Top-hat edge ties.** The 10 m contour grid has points exactly on the Jensen
  wake edge, `R + k*x`, for example `y = 130 m` at `x = 100 m`. The solvers
  resolve these ties differently, which gives single-cell differences up to
  about `4.6 m/s`. In the four-turbine Jensen contour there are 264 such
  points, and every cell where the three solvers still differ by more than
  `0.002 m/s` lies on a tie or a rotor plane.

Offsetting the sample lines and planes slightly from turbine planes and
wake edges would remove both from the metrics.
