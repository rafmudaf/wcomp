Model parameters and implementation notes

The windIO inputs and their software-specific mappings are listed below. Matching literature-model names do not imply identical solver, rotor-averaging, superposition, turbulence, induction, or yaw treatments.

windIO case inputs

  • velocity: jensenalpha = 0.1

FLORIS

  • velocity: jensenwe = 0.1

Uses sum-of-squares freestream deficit superposition (sosfs), the TurbineCubatureGrid, 9 points rotor grid with power linear-cubature, thrust linear-cubature averaging, and Crespo-Hernandez wake-added turbulence. Secondary steering, yaw-added recovery, transverse velocities, and active wake mixing are disabled.

FOXES

  • velocity: JensenWakek = 0.1, induction = Betz

Uses Betz induction, quadratic wind-speed superposition with a freestream (ws_quadratic_amb_target) deficit reference, grid16, 4 x 4 cell centers rotor sampling, rotor-point partial wakes, and the rotor_wd wake frame. No separate wake-added turbulence model is configured.

FOXES keeps its grid16 rotor model; the requested cubature with 9 points rotor grid is not applied.

PyWake

  • velocity: NOJDeficitk = 0.1

Uses PropagateDownwind with squared-sum superposition, CGIRotorAvg, 9 points rotor averaging, freestream (use_effective_ws=false) deficit reference, and ct2a_mom1d induction. No separate wake-added turbulence model is configured.

Uses the lower-level NOJDeficit class rather than PyWake's Jensen_1983 literature preset.

How to interpret this comparison

  • Along the hub-height streamwise line the closest pair is FLORIS vs FOXES (0.000 m/s RMSE, 0.0% NRMSE) and the furthest is FLORIS vs PyWake, at 0.41 m/s RMSE (7.9% NRMSE). For scale, the free-stream wind speed is 9.8 m/s.
  • For FLORIS vs PyWake, cross-section RMSE is 0.000 m/s at 4D past first turbine, 0.000 m/s at 1D past last turbine, 0.019 m/s at 5D past last turbine, 0.017 m/s at 10D past last turbine. NRMSE is normalized by the range of the first-listed software's own values in that plot, so it can rise with distance even as RMSE falls, because the wake profile becomes shallower.
  • The horizontal contour shows how a small RMSE can hide a large maximum error: FLORIS vs PyWake has an RMSE of 0.18 m/s but a maximum of 5.46 m/s. Those maxima come from a small fraction of grid points, found at the rotor plane (X = 0) or at the sharp edge of a wake, where the packages disagree about whether a point is inside the wake. Use RMSE for overall agreement and the maximum to locate where to look.
  • The Jensen deficit is a top hat: it drops from the full deficit to zero at the wake edge, so two grid points on either side of the edge can differ by several m/s. Expect large maximum errors in the contours even when the RMSE is small.
  • FLORIS and FOXES coincide along the hub-height streamwise line and all three packages agree in every cross-section (largest cross-section RMSE 0.019 m/s), so the FLORIS departure seen with the default settings is a configuration effect. PyWake still departs along the streamwise line (0.41 m/s RMSE), at locations the cross-sections do not sample, such as the rotor planes; this page does not isolate the cause.
  • A value of 0.0000 means the two packages agree at the precision shown in the table.
  • These metrics measure how consistently the packages implement the same inputs. They are not errors against measurements, so close agreement does not show that a wake model is accurate, and differences should be read together with the implementation notes above.
